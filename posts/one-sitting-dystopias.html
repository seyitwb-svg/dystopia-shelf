7 dystopias you can finish in one sitting

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 5, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

A dystopia is one wrong idea pushed to its conclusion, and short fiction is the perfect size for exactly one idea. None of these needs a second evening. All of them will cost you the rest of the night anyway.

1. The Machine Stops · E.M. Forster (1909)

Written in 1909, and it describes your phone. Humanity lives underground in isolated cells and talks only through screens. The Machine provides everything, so it is worshipped, and wanting fresh air gets you called "unmechanical." Then the Machine begins to stop. If you only read one story on this list, make it this one: we gave it its own review.

2. The Lottery · Shirley Jackson (1948)

A small town gathers on a sunny June morning for its annual lottery. The first two-thirds read like a village picnic. The ending reframes every friendly detail that came before it and shows how a community hides its violence inside routine. English teachers have assigned it for nearly eighty years, and it still silences a room.

3. Harrison Bergeron · Kurt Vonnegut (1961)

Total equality enforced by hardware. Masks for the beautiful, radio sirens in the ears of the clever. Harrison is beautiful, clever, and strong, so they hang everything on him at once. Under ten pages, and it says more about imposed fairness than most four-hundred-page novels manage.

4. The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas · Ursula K. Le Guin (1973)

A perfect city, with one child kept in misery in a basement so the rest can stay happy. Le Guin isn't really describing a city. She's describing the contract every comfortable reader has already signed. The ones who leave Omelas walk away alone, and the story never tells you where they go.

5. I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream · Harlan Ellison (1967)

A war computer called AM has murdered all humanity except five people, whom it now tortures inside its own circuitry, forever. The bleakest entry here by a distance. The system wins completely, and the only victory left is smaller than you'd hope.

6. All Summer in a Day · Ray Bradbury (1954)

On Venus it rains for seven years straight. A class of children locks the one girl who remembers the sun in a closet, on the single day the sun comes out. Schoolyard cruelty as dystopia, and the shortest story on this list.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2026)

Disclosure: I wrote this one. The longest entry here, about ninety minutes, one sitting by design. A gravedigger works a town where the sick get one violent hour a day on no fixed schedule, and a stranger offers him the one thing the world ran out of: a friend. ~68 pages, free on Kindle Unlimited. Amazon. Last on the list because the six above are the shelf it wants to earn a place on.

Where to start

One story, maximum damage: The Lottery. Want to feel your phone watching you? The Machine Stops. Want the bleakest possible hour? I Have No Mouth. Then the indie novella for dessert.
