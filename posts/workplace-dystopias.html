6 dystopias where the job is the trap

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 8, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

The scariest dystopias do not need a tyrant. They need a supervisor, a rota, and a signature line at the bottom of a form. These six novels put the workplace at the center of the machine — the seventh puts a whole town on shift.

1. The Factory · Hiroko Oyamada (2019)

Three workers take jobs at a factory so large it has its own ecosystem — and their tasks quietly dissolve any sense of what the work is for. Oyamada's deadpan makes the horror procedural: nobody is threatened, nobody is told the point, and eventually nobody can leave. Published 2019.

2. The Circle · Dave Eggers (2013)

A dream tech job becomes total surveillance wearing a wellness smile — transparency metrics, mandatory sharing, and a campus that never wants you to log off. Eggers' satire of Silicon Valley reads less exaggerated every year. Published 2013.

3. Autonomous · Annalee Newitz (2017)

A pharma pirate reverse-engineers patented drugs while a military robot tracks her across a world where people — and machines — can be patented property. The dystopia here is IP law taken literally: ownership of molecules, then of workers. Published 2017.

4. The New Me · Halle Butler (2019)

A temp worker spirals through meaningless office days narrated with surgical contempt — for the job, for herself, for the idea that employment should mean something. The smallest-scale dystopia on this list and maybe the truest one. Published 2019.

5. Temporary · Hilary Leichter (2020)

A woman drifts through absurd gig after gig — assistant, barnacle, replacement body — in a world that is all employment and no work. Leichter's fable logic makes the gig economy feel like folklore. Published 2020.

6. Severance · Ling Ma (2018)

An office drone keeps going to work after the apocalypse arrives, because routine is the last job left. Ma's apocalypse is boring on purpose — the fever empties the world, but the commute and the desk remain, which is the point. Published 2018.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2025)

Disclosure: I wrote My Brother on the Moon. A 68-page dystopia where the sickness employs every citizen for one hour a day, wages in flesh. A gravedigger whose timesheet just cleared; a stranger it never hired. $2.99, KU: amazon.com/dp/B0G1KNPF4X.

FAQ

What is a workplace dystopia?

A dystopia where the instrument of control is the job itself — the rota, the badge, the timesheet — rather than a police state. The system does not need guards; it needs employees.

What should I read after Severance by Ling Ma?

The Factory for dissolved purpose, The New Me for temp-work despair, Temporary for a world that is all employment and no work.

Is there a short workplace dystopia?

My Brother on the Moon runs about 68 pages — the sickness employs every citizen for one hour a day, and the wages come in flesh.
