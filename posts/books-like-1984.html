7 books like 1984 (dystopias of surveillance and managed minds)

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 7, 2026

George Orwell's Oceania is still the template: a state that rewrites the past and watches the present. Seven books carry the argument forward.

We by Yevgeny Zamyatin (1921)

Written in 1921 and first published in English in 1924, it stayed banned in the Soviet Union until 1988. Orwell reviewed it for Tribune in 1946 and ranked it above Brave New World; the debt Oceania owes it is visible page for page.

The One State keeps its citizens in glass houses so the watching is total, and D-503's illness is a soul.

Brave New World by Aldous Huxley (1932)

Orwell feared a boot on a human face; Huxley feared a population too entertained to feel the boot. Engineered castes kept happy by a drug for every edge. 1984 controls through fear; the World State controls through appetite, and the decades since make Orwell's win harder to claim.

Fahrenheit 451 by Ray Bradbury (1953)

The censorship half of 1984's nightmare drawn to its conclusion. Books burn because a public trained on endless entertainment stopped defending them; the state only had to keep the matches dry.

451 is the ignition point of paper, and the horror of the book is how voluntary the surrender was.

The Trial by Franz Kafka (1925)

Published posthumously, before totalitarianism had its vocabulary. Josef K. is arrested one morning for a crime that is never named, by a court that exists everywhere and nowhere.

The case is a labyrinth with no verdict in it; the execution arrives anyway.

The Handmaid's Tale by Margaret Atwood (1985)

Gilead runs on the same fuel as Oceania: policed language and spies in every room. Atwood put a woman's body at the center of the state; Offred survives by narrating what the regime wants unrecorded.

The detail that lingers is authorial: Atwood has said she included nothing that had not already happened somewhere.

The Memory Police by Yoko Ogawa (1994)

On an island where things disappear category by category, from roses to limbs, the Memory Police enforce the forgetting and hunt the few who still remember.

Ogawa's tyranny works through compliance, through the way people learn not to miss what is gone.

My Brother on the Moon by Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (68 pages)

Disclosure: I wrote this one. In Ventenstein the pressure comes from a virus: once a day the infected get one hour in which they kill, and the hour drifts. Killing stays illegal. The city tolerates a private trade in bounties anyway. Auzel the gravedigger buries the dead and carries a bounty on his own head.

If what you wanted from Orwell was a small conscience inside a system that has decided the unforgivable is ordinary, that is the book I wrote. About 68 pages and free on Kindle Unlimited; it is free for all readers October 10-11. amazon.com

FAQ

Which book is most similar to 1984?

We by Yevgeny Zamyatin. Orwell reviewed it for Tribune in 1946 and built Oceania on its blueprint: numbered citizens and a diary kept as rebellion.

Is 1984 still relevant?

The mechanisms it described keep resurfacing in new packaging: rewritten history and surveilled language on a permanent emergency footing, which is why a novel from 1949 still sells like a new release.

Which of these is shortest?

My Brother on the Moon at about 68 pages; We and The Trial each run under 250.
