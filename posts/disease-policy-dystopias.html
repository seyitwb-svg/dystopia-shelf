7 dystopias where sickness becomes policy

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 7, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

These are the books where the disease doesn't just kill — it gets an administration. Quarantine permits, rationed hours, paperwork for the dead: the plague arrives with forms attached. Six essentials, plus one indie novella where the state paperwork is the sickness itself.

1. The Plague · Albert Camus (1947)

Oran seals its gates and the town learns that a bacillus can run a bureaucracy better than any ministry: ledgers of the dead and permits for grief. Dr. Rieux keeps working not because it will win but because stopping is the one thing the plague would call agreement. Published 1947.

2. Severance · Ling Ma (2018)

Shen Fever turns nostalgia into a terminal condition — the infected just repeat their routines until they stop. Candace Chen keeps showing up at her Manhattan office through the apocalypse, which is exactly the joke: the market's survival instinct outlives civilization. Published 2018.

3. The Fireman · Joe Hill (2016)

A spore called Dragonscale tattoos its hosts in gold before burning them from the inside; the infected learn that panic is a policy tool. Harper discovers a commune that turned the disease into a discipline. Published 2016.

4. A Beginning at the End · Mike Chen (2020)

Six years after a global pandemic kills most of the planet, San Francisco runs on quarantine permits and social-credit landlords. Four strangers collide inside the paperwork of recovery. The apocalypse here is administrative as much as viral. Published 2020.

5. Station Eleven · Emily St. John Mandel (2014)

Twenty years after the Georgia Flu ends the world in weeks, a Shakespeare troupe tours the settlements between the dead towns. Mandel's point lands sideways: the plague was quick; what people build around the hole it left is the story. Published 2014.

6. The End of October · Lawrence Wright (2020)

A novel written before COVID that reads like a leak: an epidemiologist chases the Kongoli flu out of Mecca while governments improvise borders and quarantines in real time. The procedural detail is the thriller. Published 2020.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2025)

Disclosure: I wrote My Brother on the Moon. Here the policy memo is biological: one hour of infection falls on every citizen daily, never two days running at the same one. Auzel totals other people's graves; this week the invoice came home to him. Dexs files nothing — and nobody in town can price him. ~68 pages, KU: amazon.com/dp/B0G1KNPF4X.

FAQ

What are the best dystopias about disease?

Camus' The Plague is the classic, Severance is the sharpest modern take, and Station Eleven is the most humane. For a short indie entry, My Brother on the Moon runs about 68 pages.

Which pandemic dystopia should I read first?

Start with Severance if you want the satire, Station Eleven for the elegy, or The End of October for the thriller. The Plague is the slowest and the most philosophical.

Are there short plague dystopias?

Yes. The Plague is already compact, and My Brother on the Moon runs about 68 pages — a pandemic dystopia you can finish in one sitting.
