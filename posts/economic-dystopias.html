6 dystopias where the market is the state

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 8, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

Some dystopias need soldiers. These need a shareholder meeting — the company that owns your house, your food, your surname, or your corpse. Six novels where the free market stopped being a metaphor, plus one 68-page indie where the economy runs on graves.

1. The Space Merchants · Frederik Pohl & C.M. Kornbluth (1952)

Advertising agencies run the government and the product launches are foreign policy. Mitch Courtenay has to sell a colony nobody wants to live in. Every page of this 1952 novel knows something about attention economics it took the internet seventy years to learn. Published 1952.

2. Jennifer Government · Max Barry (2003)

Employees take their employer's name as a surname; the police are contractors paid per arrest. A single marketing stunt leaves bodies in a store, and the investigation is where the satire turns sharp. Barry wrote corporate personhood as a thriller. Published 2003.

3. Market Forces · Richard K. Morgan (2004)

Executives duel for contracts in armored cars on public highways — the company that wins the crash wins the deal. Chris Faulkner keeps climbing a ladder where the rungs are collision footage. Morgan applies noir logic to shareholder value. Published 2004.

4. Gladiator-at-Law · Frederik Pohl & C.M. Kornbluth (1955)

Two men get squeezed by the housing monopoly from different ends: Norvell Bligh, a game-spectacle designer, is tricked out of his job and his company housing and dumped in the slum of Belly Rave; Charles Mundin, a lawyer barred from the lucrative work, takes the case against G.M.L. itself. Pohl and Kornbluth again, hungrier this time. Published 1955.

5. The Windup Girl · Paolo Bacigalupi (2009)

Calorie companies own the food supply and write the genetic code to match. In a drowned Bangkok, everyone from the refugee to the king's envoy is negotiating with biology as a balance sheet. Emiko just wants to stop being inventory. Published 2009.

6. New York 2140 · Kim Stanley Robinson (2017)

Half of Manhattan is under water and the real-estate market doesn't blink. From floor traders to the crew of an activist airship, everyone treats the drowned city as an argument about who should pay for the future. Robinson makes flood insurance a thriller engine. Published 2017.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2025)

Disclosure: I wrote My Brother on the Moon. Ventenstein's market is quieter: graves are the local currency, and last week the man who runs the cemetery's accounts went on the rate card himself. Dexs never made it on. ~68 pages, KU: amazon.com/dp/B0G1KNPF4X.

FAQ

What are the best dystopias about capitalism?

The Space Merchants invented the corporate dystopia in 1952; Jennifer Government and Market Forces turn it into thrillers, and The Windup Girl pushes it into biotech. Each one treats the market itself as the state.

Which corporate dystopia should I read first?

Jennifer Government is the fastest read — satirical, short, and immediately recognizable. The Space Merchants if you want the origin point, Market Forces if you want it brutal.

Are there short economic dystopias?

My Brother on the Moon runs about 68 pages — a bounty economy where graves are the local currency and the cemetery's rate card just listed the gravedigger himself.
