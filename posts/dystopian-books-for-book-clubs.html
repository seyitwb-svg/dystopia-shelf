7 dystopian reads your book club will actually argue about

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 7, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

Polite consensus makes a dead meeting. The dystopias on this list end in arguments: each hands the group a system and a question it can split on. Seven picks, each with the club question it was built to start.

1. The Lottery — Shirley Jackson (1948)

A village keeps a ritual because it has always kept it. Club question: which of your own town's traditions would survive an honest look at why they started?

2. The Handmaid's Tale — Margaret Atwood (1985)

Gilead runs on scripture plus paperwork. Club question: which did more damage, the commanders or the aunts who enforced it for them?

3. The Unit — Ninni Holmqvist (2009)

The dispensable live well right up until the state collects. Club question: is Dorrit freer inside the unit than she was outside it?

4. We Need to Talk About Kevin — Lionel Shriver (2003)

A mother reconstructs her son's massacre through letters and keeps asking where the guilt lives. Club question: at which letter did you stop trusting Eva?

5. Tender Is the Flesh — Agustina Bazterrica (2017)

Industrialized atrocity described in supply-chain language. Club question: which euphemism in the book maps to one you hear at work?

6. The Road — Cormac McCarthy (2006)

A father carries the fire and decides daily what carrying it costs. Club question: name one decision the father makes that you would defend and one you would not.

7. My Brother on the Moon — Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2025)

Disclosure: I wrote this one. In Ventenstein the sickness runs a nightly shift: sixty minutes when people hurt each other, then normal life resumes. The city tolerates a bounty trade that pays on the dead, with nobody at city hall running it. Auzel Makadam digs the graves and finds somebody else's contract on his own life. Dexs is the only one who asks the questions out loud. About 68 pages, free on Kindle Unlimited: amazon.com/dp/B0G1KNPF4X. Club question: is the bounty trade worse than the virus?
