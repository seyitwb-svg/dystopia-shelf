7 plague dystopias: when the world gets sick

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 5, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

The dystopia that needs no dictator. A pathogen is a system nobody voted for, and watching what people build around it is the genre's oldest experiment.

1. The Plague · Albert Camus (1947)

Oran, locked down by rats and bureaucracy. Camus treats the epidemic as an existential audit: who keeps working, who prays, who profits, who holds the door. The ur-text of every locked-city novel that followed.

2. Earth Abides · George R. Stewart (1949)

A geography student survives a plague that empties America, then watches knowledge erode across three generations of survivors. The disease is over by page twenty; the book is about what civilization does without its memory.

3. The Stand · Stephen King (1978)

A weaponized flu kills most of the species, and the survivors sort themselves into two camps around two dreams. Sprawling, dated, unputdownable. The plague here is just the sorting mechanism for a much older fight.

4. Blindness · José Saramago (1995)

A city goes blind overnight, one person at a time, and the quarantined descend into organized savagery within days. Written in run-on sentences that refuse you a place to breathe. The scariest entry on this list because every cruelty is plausible.

5. Zone One · Colson Whitehead (2011)

After the zombie plague, Manhattan is being reclaimed block by block by teams of civilian sweepers. Whitehead writes the apocalypse as municipal grief work: forms, protocols, and the memories that keep leaking in.

6. Severance · Ling Ma (2018)

Shen Fever turns the infected into loops of their most repeated gestures, and the millennial narrator keeps going to her office job because routine is the only immunity she has. The plague novel as a critique of work itself.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2026)

Disclosure: I wrote this one. The virus here doesn't kill the body; it rents it. One hour a day the infected turn murderous, the rest of the day everyone pretends. A gravedigger in a ruined city, and the stranger who becomes his first friend since the world ended. ~68 pages, free on Kindle Unlimited. Amazon. Last because the six above built the shelf it sits on.

Where to start

The foundation: The Plague. The bleakest: Blindness. The one that explains your job: Severance. Then the indie that changed the mechanic.
