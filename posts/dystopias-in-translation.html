7 dystopias originally written in another language

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 5, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

English didn't invent the nightmare. Some of the bleakest futures were written in Russian, German, Japanese, Spanish and Swedish, and translation makes them no quieter.

1. We · Yevgeny Zamyatin (1924, Russian)

The foundation document of the whole genre, written before Orwell was out of school. The One State numbers its citizens and walls them behind glass. It schedules intimacy, and calls the soul a disease to be surgically removed. Orwell built on it openly. Huxley swore he never read it, and readers have argued about it since.

2. The Wall · Marlen Haushofer (1963, German)

An Austrian woman wakes in a hunting lodge sealed behind an invisible wall, possibly the last person alive. No villain, no explanation, just a diary of survival with her dog, cat and cow. The quietest apocalypse in any language.

3. Battle Royale · Koushun Takami (1999, Japanese)

A class of junior high students is transported to an island and ordered to kill each other in a government "Program." The premise has been copied endlessly; the original novel is colder and more procedural than any of the copies.

4. The Memory Police · Yoko Ogawa (1994, Japanese)

On an unnamed island, objects disappear one by one: roses, birds, photographs. Once they're gone, the islanders can't even remember what they were. A novelist hides her still-remembering editor in a secret room. Subtraction as totalitarianism.

5. Tender Is the Flesh · Agustina Bazterrica (2017, Spanish)

After a virus makes animal meat lethal, the state legalizes processing humans for food. Bazterrica writes the slaughterhouse economy with chilling administrative calm. The most disturbing book on this list, by a clear margin.

6. The Unit · Ninni Holmqvist (2006, Swedish)

Unmarried, childless adults over fifty are sent to a comfortable facility where they donate organs until their final contribution. The horror is how pleasant the prison is: spa, friends, art classes, while the ledger waits for your last lung.

7. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (2026, German)

Disclosure: I wrote this one. Written in German first, translated to English. A gravedigger in a ruined city where a virus requisitions one killing hour a day, and the stranger who becomes his first friend. ~68 pages, free on Kindle Unlimited. Amazon. Last because the six above are the company it wants to keep.

Where to start

The genre's source code: We. The quietest ruin: The Wall. The one you'll need to put down: Tender Is the Flesh. Then the new indie in the same tradition.
