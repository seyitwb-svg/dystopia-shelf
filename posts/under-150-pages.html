9 dystopian stories and novellas under 150 pages

Reading list · By Max, the reader behind Dystopia Shelf · Last updated: October 6, 2026

← Dystopia Shelf

Dystopias do not need 500 pages to ruin your week. Some of the sharpest ones are short enough to read on a long lunch break, and the compression makes them hit harder. Nine short books and stories below, all under 150 pages, all worth your time.

1. The Machine Stops · E. M. Forster (~30 pages)

Written in 1909. People live alone in underground cells and talk to each other only through the Machine that runs everything. It reads like it was written about us.

2. The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas · Ursula K. Le Guin (~10 pages)

A utopia with a price. Le Guin describes a perfect city and the one suffering child it depends on, then asks the only question that matters: do you stay?

3. Harrison Bergeron · Kurt Vonnegut (~8 pages)

The year is 2081 and everyone is finally equal, enforced by handicaps on anyone who is too smart or too strong. Eight pages long and brutal about it.

4. Anthem · Ayn Rand (~100 pages)

A collectivist society where the word "I" does not exist. One man rediscovers it. Whatever you think of Rand's politics, the premise lands.

5. The Pedestrian · Ray Bradbury (~5 pages)

A man walks through a silent city at night, alone, because everyone else is inside watching screens. Published in 1951. It takes a few minutes to read and stays much longer.

6. Repent, Harlequin! Said the Ticktockman · Harlan Ellison (~15 pages)

A dystopia about punctuality, where being late is a crime the state measures in stolen minutes of your life. Loud and weird, with a title nobody forgets.

7. The Country of the Blind · H. G. Wells (~20 pages)

A traveler finds a valley where everyone is blind and sight is a defect. Technically more parable than dystopia, but the inversion holds up.

8. The Girl Who Was Plugged In · James Tiptree Jr. (~50 pages)

A 1973 story about a woman whose mind remotely runs a manufactured celebrity body. Influenced Neuromancer and predicted influencer culture decades early.

9. My Brother on the Moon · Maximilian Coolheit Vorfahrenheit (~68 pages)

Full disclosure: this one is mine. A novella set in Ventenstein, where the sickness leaves each of the sick a daily window in which they can kill — and the window keeps moving, so nobody can schedule around it. Auzel the gravedigger buries the dead with a bounty on his own head. It is the least-known book on this list. Amazon.

Where to start

If you only read one: The Machine Stops. It is free on Project Gutenberg. If you want the newest: mine is on Amazon.
